1. Executive Vision
Federal courts need operational tools that reduce manual reconciliation without replacing judicial judgment or the official case-management record. This vision proposes two interoperable modules: an Evidentiary Tracking System for courtroom exhibit control and a Speedy Trial Tracker for transparent deadline monitoring. The modules can operate independently, share a common security and audit foundation, and integrate with existing court systems where authorized.

1.2 Product Boundary

 The suite supports court operations. It does not decide admissibility, make judicial findings, interpret disputed law, or replace the official docket.


 The Evidentiary Tracking module manages metadata, workflow, status, custody, reconciliation, and secure access. Storage of exhibit files is configurable because local rules and exhibit types may require different custodians.


 The Speedy Trial module calculates and presents candidate clock periods from configured rules and case events. Authorized court users remain responsible for validation and official determinations.


 Any artificial intelligence features are assistive. They propose classifications, detect discrepancies, or summarize records, but do not issue rulings or final legal conclusions.

3. Evidentiary Tracking System
3.1 What the System Does
The Evidentiary Tracking System maintains a secure, real-time ledger of proposed, offered, admitted, rejected, withdrawn, substituted, sealed, and returned exhibits. It connects exhibit metadata to the case, proceeding, offering party, objections, rulings, storage location, custody actions, and post-trial disposition. The system may reference or store digital objects depending on court policy and integration design.

 System Behavior
 Human Control


Pretrial exhibit intake
 Receives structured exhibit lists and, when permitted, digital files; validates required metadata and flags duplicates or unsupported formats.
 Clerk or deputy accepts, rejects, or requests correction.

Exhibit ledger
 Assigns or validates identifiers and maintains current status, party, description, proceeding, confidentiality, and location.
 Court controls numbering conventions and official status.

Real-time courtroom logging
 Records offers, objections, rulings, withdrawals, substitutions, timestamps, and notes during proceedings.
 Deputy records actions; judge controls rulings.

Custody and location
 Tracks possession, transfer, return, storage requirements, receipts, and responsible custodian.
 Authorized user confirms each transfer.

Quality control
 Compares party lists, deputy logs, admitted lists, jury packages, and disposition records to identify discrepancies.
 User resolves each exception with rationale.

Jury review package
 Builds a controlled package containing only items authorized for jury review, with access and session controls.
 Court authorizes package contents and release.

Post-trial closeout
 Produces an exportable exhibit list, return/retention tasks, receipts, and an appeal-ready audit package.
 Clerk or deputy certifies closeout.



3.2 How It Is Used
1.
 Case setup: Clerk staff create or synchronize the matter, proceeding, parties, security designations, exhibit rules, and numbering scheme.

2.
 Pretrial preparation: Attorneys submit proposed exhibit metadata and files where allowed. The system validates completeness and places exceptions in a court review queue.

3.
 Courtroom operation: The courtroom deputy opens the session ledger. As exhibits are offered, the deputy records status and the judge's ruling. Chambers can view the same current record.

4.
 Jury review: After court authorization, the system assembles admitted electronic exhibits into a restricted package and excludes rejected, withdrawn, or sealed items not authorized for review.

5.
 Reconciliation: At recess, day-end, and trial close, the system compares the live ledger against source lists and requires resolution of discrepancies.

6.
 Disposition: The system records return, retention, transfer, or secure storage actions and creates receipts and an auditable closeout record.

4. Speedy Trial Tracker
4.1 What the System Does
The Speedy Trial Tracker creates a transparent timeline of events relevant to statutory and court-plan deadlines. It ingests or receives docket events, identifies candidate trigger and exclusion periods using a versioned rule package, calculates running time, and presents the result with source events and explanations. It is a decision-support and risk-monitoring system, not a substitute for judicial findings or legal review.

 System Behavior
 Human Control

Event ingestion
 Receives docket events and orders from CM/ECF or structured entry and maps them to configured event categories.
 Court validates event mappings and resolves unmapped events.

Candidate exclusion engine
 Suggests periods associated with motions, competency proceedings, continuances, interlocutory matters, or other configured categories.
 Authorized reviewer accepts, modifies, or rejects each candidate period.

Clock calculation
 Displays elapsed includable time, excluded periods, remaining time, and calculation date.
 Final status depends on confirmed events and findings.

Continuance findings check
 Flags continuance records that lack required structured findings or supporting order references.
 Judge and chambers determine legal sufficiency.

Alerts and escalation
 Generates configurable alerts as remaining time crosses court-defined thresholds or unresolved events persist.
 Court controls recipients, cadence, and thresholds.

Explanation and audit
 Shows the event, rule reference, period, status, reviewer, and reason behind each calculation component.
 Users can review and document overrides.

7.1 Design Principles

 National core, local configuration: common product behavior with court-approved local rules and workflows.


 Human-in-command: system recommendations remain reviewable and reversible by authorized users.


 Explain every consequential result: source events, rule version, calculation steps, status, and reviewer are visible.


 Courtroom speed: critical interactions use minimal steps, large targets, keyboard support, and rapid recovery from interruption.


 Authoritative-source discipline: imported data retains source identifiers; local changes do not silently overwrite the official docket.


 Accessibility and usability: interfaces are designed for Section 508 conformance and demanding courtroom conditions.


 API-first interoperability: integrations are isolated behind controlled services so source systems can evolve.

10.2 Working Assumptions

 The initial market is federal district courts, with possible later adaptation for defender organizations or other courts.


 The official case-management and docket system remains the authoritative case record.


 Local rules and court plans vary, so national deployment requires configuration and governance rather than one rigid workflow.


 Judges and authorized court personnel retain final authority over rulings, findings, exclusions, corrections, and official records.


 Production design will be based on validated interfaces and approved security, privacy, records, accessibility, and continuity requirements.
