<objective>
Close the three task-only producers, build the retention disposition sweep Phase 1 handed to Phase 3 by
name, and make the outbound refusal unroutable by refusing the grant itself.

Purpose: plan 03-09's extension of `ExceptionsService.raise()` covers every exception-backed task with one
edit. CONTEXT names the remaining three producers that have no exception behind them and therefore need a
direct `TasksService.create()` at their own call site: **pending entitlement grants** (Phase 1, plan
01-08), **pending config publishes** (Phase 2, plan 02-08), and **retention due-for-disposition**.

The third of those does not exist yet, and building it is a **deliberately inherited handoff, not scope
creep**. Phase 1 wrote: *"The scheduled sweep that generates due-for-disposition tasks waits for the Work
Queue in Phase 3. Building it now would only accumulate a backlog nobody can action for two phases."* The
Work Queue now exists, so the stated precondition is satisfied. Leaving it out means a court has retention
schedules, a disposition guard and a legal retention obligation, with nothing that ever tells anyone an
item is due — a compliance control that silently does nothing.

The last piece belongs here because `grants.service.ts` is the file: **no user may be granted
`docket_outbound` in v1**. Plan 03-02 made the request path an unconditional deny; this makes the grant
path refuse too, so "nothing can write to the docket in v1" is a property with two independent proofs
rather than one.

Output: the disposition sweeper and its job, task creation at the two existing producer sites, the
non-grantable refusal, and three test files.
</objective>
